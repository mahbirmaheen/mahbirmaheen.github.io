About Me

I am an undergraduate student in Materials and Metallurgical Engineering (MME) and a physics educator with a strong interest in computational materials science and computationally driven materials research. For over three years, I have taught physics to high school and engineering students, emphasizing the development of deep conceptual understanding through fundamental physical principles. Concurrently, I employ computational methods in materials research. My scholarly interests include Density Functional Theory (DFT), Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, and Machine Learning (ML) applications in materials science. I focus on understanding material dynamics at the atomic scale and integrating first-principles simulations with data-driven predictions of material properties.
Currently, I am involved in projects to predict material properties using various machine learning approaches. My objective is to integrate physics-based simulations with Machine Learning to accelerate materials discovery and design. I intend to pursue graduate research in materials science and contribute to my country and society by the advancement of high-quality science exploration.

Research

Publication: Accelerated Discovery and Stability Prediction of Lead-Free Halide Perovskites via Physics-Informed Machine Learning

DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18776434

Abstract: Metal halide perovskites have emerged as promising candidates for advanced photovoltaic and optoelectronic devices; however, their commercial deployment is significantly limited by the toxicity and chemical instability of lead-based compositions. All-inorganic lead-free alternatives, particularly cesium tin mixed-halide perovskites, (CsSn(ClxBryI(1-x-y))3) provide a viable pathway to environmentally benign and thermally stable materials . However, identifying experimentally realizable compositions remains challenging due to the vast combinatorial compositional space and the persistent discrepancy between theoretical stability and practicalsynthesis.
This study develops a physics-informed and explainable machine learning framework to predict the synthesizability and thermodynamic stability of mixed-halide tin perovskites . A Positive-Unlabeled learning strategy, utilizing transductive bagging and decision tree classifiers, estimates synthesis probability based on literature-derived data. A 76-dimensional descriptor set, incorporating compositional, elemental, structural, and density functional theory (DFT)-derived thermodynamic parameters, is constructed . Gradient boosting regression modelsfurther predict macroscopic properties, while SHAP analysis provides physical interpretability of the prediction.
The model identifies a limited subset of compositions with a high probability of successful synthesis and demonstrates that the advanced tolerance factor (𝜏), B-site atomic characteristics, and local lattice distortion are dominant stability descriptors. Machine-learned atomic potentials enable effective mapping of temperature-driven structural phase properties, overcoming the limitations of ab initio molecular dynamics . This framework bridges computational predictions and experimental feasibility, establishing a general data-driven methodology that accelerates the discovery pipeline for stable, non-toxic, lead-free perovskite materials.

Computational Projects

Data-Driven Prediction of Concrete Compressive Strength (Ensemble Machine Learning)

I developed a machine learning model to predict concrete compressive strength based on composition and curing parameters using the UCI experimental dataset. Ensemble models performed much better than linear regression (R2 = 0.63), with XGBoost achieving an R2 of about 0.93. The model also highlighted meaningful trends, like how curing age and the water-cement ratio affect strength.
